## TV position and seating

Keep the TV on the solid left wall, perpendicular to the front window and patio slider. This avoids placing it directly opposite the large front glazing. It does not guarantee a reflection-free screen: angled daylight, the kitchen glazing across the open connection, and room lighting can still cause glare.

Rotate the 7.5 × 3 ft primary sofa to face the TV directly. Its footprint is x=9.5–12.5 ft, y=6.5–14 ft from the front-left corner. The illustrated central seated eye point at x=11, y=10.25 gives about 10.4 ft to a screen plane at x=0.65 ft. This is a seating assumption, not a measured person's position. Outer seats have oblique viewing angles and should be tested.

The secondary 6 × 3 ft sofa moves to x=3.5–9.5, y=16–19. It is for conversation and occasional viewing; its occupants do not face the screen directly. If both sofas must be equally good for long TV sessions, replace the secondary sofa with two movable chairs facing the screen rather than pretending its sideways view is comfortable.

Use a 6 ft wide × 1 ft 3 in deep console, with its long dimension along the left wall. A 65-inch screen is a provisional size to test, not a purchase recommendation; compare a taped outline against a 55-inch option from the actual seats. Check screen/stand dimensions, mount loads and ventilation for the selected model. Start screen-centre positioning around the occupants' seated eye level; measure the real sofa and users before fixing it. Do not place it near ceiling level or over a tall decorative panel.

## Control glare before paying for a different TV

- Give the 8 ft front living window its own adjustable blind or lined curtain. Sheer fabric alone may leave the screen too bright in daytime.
- Give the 7 ft patio slider a separate curtain track so access can remain usable. Split a wide blind assembly into supplier-approved widths; do not assume an 8 ft single blind will work without support or sagging.
- Do not obstruct the slider, its handle or its clear opening with the curtain stack. Set track position and curtain parking after checking the one-foot pier between the window and slider and the wall available beside them.
- Use matte paint around the TV and a low-sheen console top; avoid a mirror or polished stone feature panel opposite the screen.
- Keep decorative lamps and downlights from reflecting toward seated viewers. Use separately switched/dimmable ambient light; ceiling fixtures still need a reflected-ceiling layout.
- The front balcony/porch may shade the glazing, but its effect depends on direction, sun angle and projection. Do not reduce glazing or deepen shading without a daylight and facade check.
- Test with the screen dark from all regular seats in morning, midday and late afternoon; check the kitchen opening as well as the front glazing. Adjust curtains and lamp positions before choosing a TV based on anti-reflection marketing.

[Sony's placement guidance](https://helpguide.sony.net/tv/nga4/v1/en-142/contents/TP1002508737.html) advises keeping light from shining directly on the screen. [Samsung's sizing guide](https://www.samsung.com/ca/support/tv-audio-video/tv-find-the-right-size-tv-for-your-room/) relates size to viewing distance. These support checking lighting and viewing distance; neither verifies this room or promises no glare.
